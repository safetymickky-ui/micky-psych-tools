# Generation Z and Alpha Psychiatry

Topic map for what has actually changed in the psychiatry of the cohorts born after 1997, and
for the methodological trap that makes almost every headline about them wrong in the same way.

> The disagreement in this field is not about facts — it is about **aggregation**. Every claim
> that fails is an average over heterogeneous people, exposures and outcomes. Every claim that
> survives specifies *whom*, *what kind of use*, *at what age*, and *on what endpoint*.

Three numbers carry the whole area. Screen time as **hours** explains ≤0.4% of variance in
wellbeing. The same exposure measured as **dyscontrol** correlates with depression at r ≈ 0.27
and anxiety at r ≈ 0.35, and with loneliness at d = 0.53. And **cyberbullying victimisation**
predicts symptoms prospectively *after adjusting total screen time away*. Dose is the weakest
measure anyone uses and the only one the public argues about.

Keep two separations attached to any figure taken from here. **Incidence vs ascertainment**:
suicide and eating disorders show incidence signals; ADHD and autism show ascertainment
signatures (a post-2020 ADHD excess concentrated in adult women from *less deprived* areas; a
5.5-fold autism identification gap between two US surveillance sites). Conflating the two is the
field's most common error. And **specification vs phenomenon**: identical datasets yield β from
−0.01 to −0.24 depending on four analytic choices, so a single-number claim in either direction
is a claim about a pipeline until the specification space is shown.
