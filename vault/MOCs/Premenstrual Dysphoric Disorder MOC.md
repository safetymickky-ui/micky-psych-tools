# Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder

Topic map for the premenstrual disorder family — PMDD as the spine, with premenstrual syndrome
and premenstrual exacerbation of an underlying disorder treated as distinct entities rather than
as degrees of the same thing. Two findings organise everything filed here.

> The disorder is an abnormal response to normal hormone change, not a hormone abnormality.

Identical exogenous steroid produces opposite behavioural consequences in affected and unaffected
women; suppress ovulation and cyclicity disappears, give either steroid back and it returns — only
in the susceptible. Allopregnanolone levels are broadly normal; what is abnormal is the loss of
the cycle-dependent retuning of GABA-A sensitivity. That is why progesterone supplementation fails,
why 5α-reductase inhibition and allopregnanolone antagonism are both coherent strategies, and why
adding neurosteroid tone (zuranolone) and removing it (sepranolone) cannot both be right about a
*level* but can both be right about *plasticity*.

> The instrument decides the answer, and most PMDD diagnosed in ordinary care is not PMDD.

Prospectively confirmed prevalence is 3.2% against 7.7% provisional, and 1.6% in community samples
with confirmation. Of self-referred women with a retrospective PMDD complaint who then chart two
cycles, 19% have PMDD and 58% have no cyclical disorder at all. Two months of daily rating is the
top-ranked auditable standard in every consensus document — and between 6.6% and 11.5% of surveyed
clinicians actually do it. Keep the ascertainment method attached to any prevalence figure taken
from here; the difference between a screener and a diary is larger than most differences between
countries.
