# Oral Health and Psychiatry

Topic map for the two-way traffic between the mouth and the mind. The through-line of
everything filed here is that the two arrows are not equally supported:

> Psychiatry → mouth is large, causal and actionable. Mouth → psychiatry is real,
> small, and confounded by the life course that produced both.

The forward arrow (oral disease as a *cause* of psychiatric or cognitive illness) survives
every observational pooling at RR ≈ 1.2–1.3 and then fails every design that can break it —
Mendelian randomization in two independent cohorts, the childhood-cognition life-course
account, and the single adequately powered intervention trial. For depression it does not
even survive the move from cross-sectional to prospective designs. The reverse arrow needs
no such defence: severe mental illness carries 2.8× the odds of total tooth loss.

Keep the direction attached to any number taken from here. Almost every apparent paradox in
this literature dissolves once you ask which arrow the study actually measured.
