# Cognitive Enhancement and Supplements

Topic map for whether over-the-counter compounds change cognition, and in whom. The through-line
of everything filed here is one finding:

> These compounds correct deficits; they do not enhance an intact system.

Every agent with a positive pooled estimate turns out to owe it to a subgroup with a deficit —
vitamin D in the deficient (*g* = 0.480 vs 0.128 overall), omega-3 in those with low n-3 status,
creatine in adults aged 66–76 (SMD 0.88) but not 11–31 (SMD 0.03). The agents with adequately
powered trials in replete populations — ginkgo, B vitamins, cocoa flavanols — return precise
nulls. Keep the population attached to any number taken from here: almost none of this evidence
was generated in healthy adults under 60.

The topic has since widened past supplements to the whole enhancement field — stimulants,
eugeroics, transcranial stimulation, psychedelic microdosing, cognitive training, exercise and
sleep — and the through-line survives the widening intact. Two further regularities now sit
alongside it: **the subjective effect and the objective effect dissociate** (belief that one has
taken a stimulant produces the felt enhancement with none of the measured one), and **effects
that survive publication-bias correction are almost always the combination arms**, where the
active ingredient is the training rather than the drug or the current.
